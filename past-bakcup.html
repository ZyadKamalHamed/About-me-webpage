Past

Schooling | 16 - 18

As a kid, we moved around a lot. The house that I currenlty reside in is the 15th to date. I've been to three primary schools, all of which I thrived in academically.

Come highschool, I became less interested with education, and due to being raised by a single mother, I naturally gravitated towards hip-hop culture and began to fantisise about becoming an NBA player or famous lyracist that could one day use his riches to pay his mother back for all she had done for him.

Given my height never broke 5"10, I figured music may be the more realistic route to achieve my goals, and if I was going to be like my idol "Kendrick Lamar", by 18 I'd be rich, famous, and touring the world. So, at 16, I stayed home from school to release my first single on souncloud

At 17, to pursue music further, I decided to enrol in a tafe course labelled "Techncial production", since my high-school lacked the funding to offer a music program, As it was a full-time course, I'd need to drop out of high-school to partake. I was torn, I had just got to year 11, and for the first time in a long time I was taking my education seriously again, but ultimately, the pursiasion of my favourite Yotuber speaking about dropping out of college to pursure their dream, backed by my mothers suppport, I decided to go all in on music.

Music | 18 - 24

Fast forward to 18, (still not rich and famous). I graduated The Grove Studios with a diploma of technical production and released my first E.P. "GLORY". By this time, ABC had released a documentary on local Hiphop artists in Sydney, finally, people that were like me. I would frequeenlty visited Sydney to attend showcases, gigs, and even art showcases to be a part of the scene. Come this age, My mother had also decided she was sick of the coastal life. Being a city girl at heart with two kids that had outgrown their little coastal town, she decided it's time the three of us move to Sydney. This was my time to make a name in the scene. A lot happened from then, here's the spark notes

  • Met my producer and now long-time friend
  • joined a band with said producer as the third vocalist
  • made life-long friendshiips through music and side-jobs
  • experienced my first breakup
  • played a festival
  • realised I was getting older, and music wasn't going to get me to my goal of paying my mother back
  • Did an online course in UX Design and got my first client as a freelancer
  • Went to UTS open day at 24 years old, just to sus it out, fell in love, applied, accidentally got in

That time a big chunk of my life and youth. Sometimes I regret not starting University earlier, but I could never replace the friendships, experiences, and self-identity I gained from choosing that path, nor would I have the current urgency i have to make something from my career now. P.S. I've never seen my mother as excited as she was when I told her I was going to university aside fron the time I supriused her with a smart TV for her birthday. Bless her.

Tech bro | 24 - 27

When I first enrolled at UTS, it was in Visual Communications of Design as they offered no bachelor "UX/UI Design" but it was offered as a major in Viscom. Half a semester in this degree was enough to make me question if I had just put myself at the begining of another creative pursuit that would never allow me to reach the goal of financial freedom. After doing further research on allumni of this dgeree, the competitiveness of the industry and ceiling of earnings from a carrer in UX, I pondered if I was smart enough to do Computer Science instead to give myself more career oppurtunity. My Viscom teachers said it would be too hard, my friend in the tech industry said if i do it, don't focus on anything but AI. Anxious as ever, knowing it would be difficult, I applied to a bachelor of AI and I got in

I transferred after my first semester. I didn't know how much maths woud be involved. As somoene who dropped out in year 11 and didn't pay much attention in class before that, it meant I'd have to learn how to multiply fractions at the age of 24 leading to a requirement of relentless studying to be able to just pass what most students in my cohort could HD without studying a day for. But I loveed it. There was no subjectivity on my grades, 1 + 1 is always = to 2. In Viscom, it's subjective how well you understood and portayed artistic vision. I found myself really enjoying the black & white nature of a degree based in logic, there was no room for doubt, the thing I had been escaping from my career in muic, I finallly was getting rewardeded for what I knew how to to best, work hard. hard work, and that just pushed me to work even harder, outside of the subjects based in mathematics, I almost always received a high distinction.

2025 I was applying for every internship under the sun, trying to learn DSA while working 5am days in the warehouse and going straight to University after. It was competitive as heck to say the least, and I still felt as if I was playing catchup to the allumni who had learned DSA in high-school. My only edge was my communication skills, given to me from my extensive life experience and confidence gained from living it. I was applying for everything and anything. Then a job came up on the UTS job board - "AI specialist" for an advertising agency. Thinking a role withiout "internship" in the tile was probably out of my league I went form what the heck, can't harm in applying to what the heck, I actually got an interview. Finally. I prepared for this interview all day and night researched wtf Image generation was, bought subscriptions, practised everything required on the JD, did countless mock interviews with Chat GPT. I even built out a business proccess map of how their business is likely set up, and where AI could help out in that existing proccess. I think that one impressed them. I came in to the office for my second interview with the Business director,creative drietor, and the head of architecure, "Reid". I remember thinking, Reid looks so cool I can't wait to work with him. I even met the CEO. I remember going to a bar to celebrate, I had taken a photo of myself at my lowest point pushing trolleys less than a year ago so that I could look back on it and see how far I'd come. After feeling really good about the interview, I went to a bar to treat myself to a glass of whiskey. I reminisced on that photo, and took a new one to compare. Shortly after, I received the job offer. Time to walk the walk. 1 year later I've trained over 50+ staff on AI literacy, created a new srvice offering for their ket clients in AI consultancy, AI production, and font-end software builds. I also created python automation scripts that saved the E&E team 39 weeks of manual labour accross all projects a year. I feel now as the work I can do it this company is slowly fading, there isn't much growth potential here for me. I've learned so much about business, design, and AI, but I'm ready to join a company that's tech first, where I can gain more technical depth and truley propel my career for the long-term.
