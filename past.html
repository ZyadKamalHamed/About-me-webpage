Past

Schooling | 16 - 18

As a kid, we moved around a lot. The house that I currently reside in is the 15th to date. I've been to three primary schools, all of which I thrived in academically. Come high school, I became less interested with education and at 16, I stayed home from school to release my first single on SoundCloud.

At 17, to pursue music further, I decided to enrol in a TAFE course labelled "Technical Production", since my high-school lacked the funding to offer a music program, As it was a full-time course, I'd need to drop out of high-school to partake. I was torn, I had just got to year 11, and for the first time in a long time I was taking my education seriously again, but ultimately, the persuasion of my favourite YouTuber speaking about dropping out of college to pursue their dream, backed by my mother's support, I decided to go all in on music.

Music | 18 - 24

I graduated The Grove Studios with a diploma of technical production and released my first E.P. "GLORY". At 18, my mother, my sister and I wanted to move to Sydney. The following are some key events that occurred from the ages of 18-24:

  • Met my former producer and now long-time friend
  • Joined a band with said producer as the third vocalist
  • Played some festivals, and toured a little
  • Realised I was getting older, and music wasn't going to get me to my financial goals
  • Did an online course in UX Design and got my first client as a freelancer
  • Went to UTS open day at 24 years old, just to sus it out, fell in love, applied, and accidentally got in

That time was a big chunk of my life and youth. Sometimes I regret not starting University earlier, but I could never replace the friendships, experiences, and self-identity I gained from choosing that path, nor would I have the current urgency I have to make something from my career now. P.S. I've never seen my mother as excited as she was when I told her I was going to university aside from the time I surprised her with a smart TV for her birthday.

Tech bro | 24 - 27

When I first enrolled at UTS, it was in Visual Communications. After my first semester, I was worried about the earning potential and career options and so I applied to transfer to a Bachelor of AI and I got in.

I didn't know how much maths would be involved. As someone who dropped out in year 11 and didn't pay much attention in class before that, it meant relentless studying to be able to pass what most students in my cohort could HD without studying a day for. But I appreciated the black & white nature of a degree based in logic, I was finally getting rewarded for hard work, and that just pushed me to work even harder.

In 2025 I was applying for every internship under the sun when a job came up on the UTS job board for an "AI specialist" at an advertising agency. I landed my first interview and prepared on overdrive, I even built out a business process map of how their business is likely set up, and where AI could help out in that existing process. I received the job offer after my second interview and that was one year ago, since then, I've trained over 50+ staff on AI literacy, created new AI service offerings for key clients, and created python automation scripts that saved the Architecture team 39 weeks of manual labour across all projects a year.
